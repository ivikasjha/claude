## Legal basis
- **[confirmed] F01** Medical Devices Rules, 2017 (MDR-2017) were notified by MoHFW as G.S.R. 78(E) on 31 January 2017 and came into force on 1 January 2018, under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940.  
  Source: AZB & Partners – Medical Devices Rules 2017 / CDSCO Medical Devices Rules page (2017-01-31) <https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/opencms/en/Acts-and-rules/Medical-Devices-Rules/> — Note: Also stated by azbpartners.com/bank/medical-devices-rules-2017 and bhattandjoshiassociates.com.
- **[likely] F02** MDR-2017 defines 'medical device' in three limbs of s.3(b) of the D&C Act 1940: (a) IVD substances, surgical dressings/bandages/staples/sutures/ligatures and blood-collection bags under s.3(b)(i); (b) mechanical contraceptives (condoms, IUDs, tubal rings), disinfectants and insecticides notified under s.3(b)(ii); (c) devices notified by the Central Government under s.3(b)(iv).  
  Source: AZB & Partners – Medical Devices Rules 2017 (2017-04-01) <https://azbpartners.com/bank/medical-devices-rules-2017> — Note: Law-firm quotation of the rule text; could not fetch the gazette PDF (egress blocked).
- **[confirmed] F03** Before 11 February 2020 only 37 categories of devices had been notified under s.3(b)(iv) and were regulated; on 11 Feb 2020 MoHFW issued two notifications: one notifying ALL medical devices under s.3(b)(iv) (effective 1 April 2020) and G.S.R. 102(E) (Medical Devices (Amendment) Rules, 2020) inserting Chapter IIIA 'Registration of certain medical devices'.  
  Source: Conventus Law – India: CDSCO to regulate all medical devices (2020-03-27) <https://conventuslaw.com/report/india-cdsco-to-regulate-all-medical-devices-in/> — Note: Corroborated by lexplosion.in/?p=122692, legitquest and cliniexperts. The definition notification is commonly cited as S.O. 648(E) but that number did not appear verbatim in results – treat the S.O. number as unverified.
- **[confirmed] F04** Chapter IIIA registration (G.S.R. 102(E), effective 1 April 2020) was voluntary for 18 months and became mandatory from 1 October 2021; the 37 pre-2020 notified device categories (Eighth Schedule annexure) were exempt from this registration because they were already licensed.  
  Source: Lexplosion – Medical Devices (Amendment) Rules 2020 (2020-02-11) <https://lexplosion.in/?p=122692> — Note: Also cliniexperts.com/medical-devices-ivd-amendment-rules-2020-india/ and legitquest.
- **[confirmed] F05** Transition deadlines for newly notified devices: Class A and B devices needed a manufacturing/import licence by 1 October 2022; Class C and D by 1 October 2023 (CDSCO circular of 12 Oct 2023 confirmed Class C/D under full licensing from 1 Oct 2023).  
  Source: Freyr – Mandatory Registration of Medical Devices in India (2023-10-12) <https://www.freyrsolutions.com/blog/mandatory-registration-of-medical-devices-in-india> — Note: Circular date from JSA Annual Healthcare Compendium 2023 (jsalaw.com).
- **[likely] F06** The draft Drugs, Medical Devices and Cosmetics Bill, 2023 (successor to the 2022 draft) would replace the Drugs and Cosmetics Act 1940 with a separate chapter for medical devices; as of mid-2023 it had not been approved by Cabinet, and in August 2026 pharma and device industry groups objected that their suggestions were not reflected in the latest overhaul draft. It has not been enacted.  
  Source: Business Standard – Health Min moots Centre, State be empowered to regulate drugs, cosmetics (2023-07-19) <https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/health-min-moots-centre-state-be-empowered-to-regulate-drugs-cosmetics-123071900418_1.html> — Note: Status in 2026 drawn from search summary of thesouthfirst.com and majmudarindia.com; exact 2026 draft title/date not verified.

## Core obligations – Essential Principles
- **[likely] F08** Every device must conform to the Essential Principles of safety and performance (MDR-2017 Rule 6) and a licence applicant submits an Essential Principles Checklist; Class A non-sterile non-measuring manufacturers self-certify compliance with the EP checklist when registering.  
  Source: TeamLease RegTech – Medical Devices (Sixth Amendment) Rules, 2022 (2022-10-14) <https://www.teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/19622/medical-devices-sixth-amendment-rules-2022> — Note: Self-certification to EP checklist confirmed; the rule number '6' is from memory (not visible in results).

## Core obligations – standards
- **[unverified] F09** Rule 7 (conformity with standards): devices must conform to BIS standards or standards notified by MoHFW; where none exists, to ISO/IEC or other international standards; otherwise to the manufacturer's validated standards.  
  Source: Medical Devices Rules 2017 (IIT Madras mirror PDF) (2017-01-31) <https://www.iitm.ac.in/sites/default/files/Others/medicaldevicesrules2017.pdf> — Note: From memory; PDF could not be fetched (egress blocked). Check Rule 7 text before use.

## Core obligations – QMS
- **[confirmed] F10** BIS has published IS 23485 'Medical Devices – QMS requirements and Essential Principles of safety & performance' by amalgamating ISO 13485:2016 with ISO 16142-1:2016 and ISO 16142-2:2017, so one standard covers both QMS and Essential Principles.  
  Source: BIS – BIS publishes IS 23485 <https://www.bis.gov.in/bis-publishes-is-23485-medical-devices-quality-management-system-requirements-and-essential-principles-of-safety-performance-for-medical-devices> — Note: Official BIS source.
- **[likely] F11** MDR-2017 requires a Quality Management System per the Fifth Schedule (modelled on ISO 13485); an ISO 13485:2016 certificate is not a statutory precondition for a licence, but CDSCO/notified-body audits expect the QMS to be aligned with ISO 13485.  
  Source: CliniExperts – ISO 13485 for Indian medical device manufacturers <https://cliniexperts.com/iso-13485-for-indian-medical-device-manufacturers-what-it-means-for-cdsco-compliance/> — Note: Consultancy source; corroborated by cyrilamarchand/conventuslaw commentary.

## Core obligations – labelling
- **[likely] F12** Chapter VI / Rule 44 labelling particulars (indelible ink on shelf pack and every outer cover): device name and identifying details; manufacturer name and manufacturing-premises address; net quantity; month and year of manufacture and expiry (or shelf life), expiry meaning last day of the month and preceded by 'Expiry date' or 'Shelf Life'; plus Rule 46/Fourth Schedule declarations.  
  Source: Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas – Decoding India's dual framework for medical device labelling (2025-02) <https://corporate.cyrilamarchandblogs.com/2025/02/double-check-decoding-indias-dual-framework-for-medical-device-labelling/> — Note: Full Rule 44 list (licence no., batch/lot, storage, sterile status, single-use, warnings, importer details) not reproduced in results.

## Core obligations – UDI
- **[likely] F15** Rule 46 (UDI): original MDR-2017 fixed 1 January 2022 as the UDI start date; the Medical Devices (Amendment) Rules 2021 (draft G.S.R. 877(E) 23 Dec 2021, final G.S.R. 918(E) 31 Dec 2021) replaced it with 'with effect from such date as the Central Government may specify', i.e. deferred 'till further orders'; no production identifier was prescribed and, as of 2026, no implementation date has been fixed.  
  Source: SCC Online – Draft Medical Devices (Amendment) Rules 2021 introduces UDI (2021-12-31) <https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2021/12/24/draft-medical-devices-amendment-rules-2021-introduces-unique-device-identification-of-the-medical-device/> — Note: Final G.S.R. 918(E)/31 Dec 2021 from simpliance.in; one result labels the draft 'S.O. 877(E)' – number type inconsistent. corpbiz.io says govt 'decided not to fix any date'.

## Core obligations – licence validity
- **[likely] F17** Rule 26: a manufacturing/import licence is perpetual, subject to payment of a retention fee (every 5 years); CDSCO has issued notices urging licence/certificate holders to pay retention fees on time to keep licences valid.  
  Source: Lexplosion – CDSCO urges timely payment of retention fees <https://lexplosion.in/stakeholders-in-medical-devices-sector-urged-by-cdsco-to-ensure-timely-payment-of-retention-fees-to-maintain-validity-of-licenses-and-certificates/> — Note: Rule 26 = perpetual licence per blog.ipleaders.in; '5 years' from memory.

## Core obligations – suspension/cancellation
- **[likely] F18** The Medical Devices (Third Amendment) Rules, 2022 inserted Rule 43A: if a licensee fails to comply with import-licence conditions or any provision of the Act/Rules, the CLA may, after show-cause, suspend the licence for a period or cancel it, wholly or for part of the devices.  
  Source: Lexplosion – MoH amends MDR 2017, prescribes suspension and cancellation of licence (2022) <https://lexplosion.in/ministry-of-health-amends-the-medical-devices-rules-2017-prescribes-suspension-and-cancellation-of-licence-for-manufacturer-or-licensee-for-failure-to-comply-with-conditions-of-an-import-license-ef/> — Note: G.S.R. number/date of the Third Amendment 2022 not captured.

## Core obligations – Class A exemption
- **[confirmed] F19** G.S.R. 777(E) of 14 Oct 2022 (Medical Devices (Sixth Amendment) Rules, 2022) created a registration-only route for Class A non-sterile, non-measuring (NSNM) devices: manufacturer uploads site and device details on the Online System for Medical Devices with an undertaking that the device is Class A NSNM and self-certifies compliance with the Essential Principles checklist and MDR-2017 – no manufacturing licence needed.  
  Source: TeamLease RegTech – Medical Devices (Sixth Amendment) Rules, 2022 (2022-10-14) <https://www.teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/19622/medical-devices-sixth-amendment-rules-2022> — Note: corpbiz.io calls the same G.S.R. the 'Fifth Amendment' – naming conflict; number and date agree.

## Clinical investigation – framework
- **[confirmed] F23** Chapter VII of MDR-2017 governs clinical investigation of investigational medical devices and clinical performance evaluation of new IVDs: apply to the CLA in Form MD-22 with Seventh Schedule documents; permission is granted in Form MD-23.  
  Source: CDSCO – Checklist Form MD-22 <https://www.cdsco.gov.in/opencms/export/sites/CDSCO_WEB/Pdf-documents/medical-device/ChecklisFormMD-22MD.pdf> — Note: Also corpbiz.io and cliniexperts.com.

## Clinical investigation – definitions
- **[likely] F24** 'Clinical investigation' = systematic study of an investigational medical device in or on human participants to assess safety, performance or effectiveness; 'pilot clinical investigation' = exploratory first-in-human study in a small group to obtain device-specific information before the pivotal study; 'pivotal clinical investigation' = confirmatory study to gather definitive safety/effectiveness evidence.  
  Source: Corpbiz – Process of application for clinical investigation under MDR 2017 <https://corpbiz.io/learning/process-of-application-for-clinical-investigation-under-mdr-2017/> — Note: One result (dezshira) states pilot = 3–5 patients, pivotal = 10–50 patients; these numbers are NOT in the rules – do not put on a slide as legal requirements.

## Clinical investigation – Seventh Schedule
- **[likely] F25** MD-22 dossier (Seventh Schedule) includes: clinical investigation plan, investigator's brochure, design-control documents, design verification & validation report, Essential Principles checklist, informed consent form, investigator undertaking, EC approval, and whether the study is pilot, pivotal or post-marketing.  
  Source: CDSCO – Checklist Form MD-22 <https://www.cdsco.gov.in/opencms/export/sites/CDSCO_WEB/Pdf-documents/medical-device/ChecklisFormMD-22MD.pdf> — Note: Checklist content as summarised in search text; PDF not fetched.

## Clinical investigation – ethics committee
- **[unverified] F26** Rule 50 of MDR-2017 requires Ethics Committee approval before a clinical investigation; the EC must be registered with the CLA (registration under the New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules 2019 – application Form CT-01, registration Form CT-02).  
  Source: Medical Devices Rules 2017 (IIT Delhi mirror PDF) (2017-01-31) <https://web.iitd.ac.in/~elangovan/ethics/doc/india-medical-devices-rules-2017.pdf> — Note: Search confirmed only that 'Ethics Committee is referred to in rule 50'; CT-01/CT-02 linkage is from memory.

## Clinical investigation – SAE, compensation, CTRI
- **[unverified] F27** During a clinical investigation: SAEs are reported by the investigator to sponsor/EC/CLA within 24 hours and analysed reports by the sponsor to the CLA within 14 days; the sponsor must provide free medical management and compensation for injury/death (formula-based); the study must be registered on CTRI before enrolment.  
  Source: Medical Devices Rules 2017 (IIT Madras mirror PDF) (2017-01-31) <https://www.iitm.ac.in/sites/default/files/Others/medicaldevicesrules2017.pdf> — Note: From memory (mirrors NDCT 2019 timelines). No search result confirmed the MDR rule numbers or days. Verify Rules 53–62 before use.

## Clinical investigation – academic study
- **[unverified] F28** MDR-2017 does not provide a stand-alone 'academic clinical study' exemption with CDSCO permission waived; the 'academic clinical trial' concept (EC approval plus intimation, CLA may object within 30 days) is in the NDCT Rules 2019 for drugs.  
  Source: Vidhi – Comments on Clinical Trials Rules (2018-03-23) <https://vidhilegalpolicy.in/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/180323_CommentsonClinicalTrialsRules.pdf> — Note: Search only surfaced the NDCT definition; whether MDR-2017 contains an equivalent clause could not be confirmed – flagged as open question.

## Clinical investigation – waiver (Rule 63)
- **[confirmed] F29** Rule 63 (Chapter VIII) governs permission to import/manufacture a device with no predicate in India (application Form MD-26, permission MD-27). The fourth proviso to Rule 63(1) lets the CLA waive local clinical investigation if the device is approved by the US, UK, Australia, Canada or Japan regulator, has been marketed there for at least 2 years, and the CLA is satisfied with safety/performance/post-market data – subject to a post-marketing clinical investigation in India.  
  Source: Operon Strategist – India waives local trials: exemption for investigational devices with EU clearance (2026) <https://operonstrategist.com/exemption-for-investigational-medical-devices-eu-approval/> — Note: Agrees with pureglobal.com and digitalhealthnews.com. 'No safety alert' wording not seen in results; CLA satisfaction with post-market vigilance data is the stated test.

## Post-market – PSUR
- **[likely] F32** Licence/permission holders for new devices must submit Periodic Safety Update Reports to the CLA every 6 months for the first 2 years after marketing approval and annually for the following 2 years.  
  Source: Navigator Global – India educational series: registration and post-market compliance <https://navigator.global/gb/library/india-educational-series-medical-device-registration-and-post-market-compliance> — Note: Rule number not captured (believed to be a condition attached to Rule 63 MD-27 permission). Single consultancy source.

## Post-market – adverse events
- **[likely] F33** Serious adverse events (death or life-threatening deterioration) must be reported by the licence holder within 15 days; Rule 38 requires any regulatory/administrative action abroad (withdrawal, restriction, cancellation) to be reported to CDSCO within 15 calendar days.  
  Source: Navigator Global – India educational series: registration and post-market compliance <https://navigator.global/gb/library/india-educational-series-medical-device-registration-and-post-market-compliance> — Note: 15-day SAE timeline corroborated by lexplosion.in MvPI article; rule number for SAE reporting (licence conditions) not captured.

## Post-market – MvPI mandate
- **[confirmed] F34** CDSCO circular dated 15 May 2024 reiterated that all medical-device licence holders must report every suspected unexpected serious adverse event to the Materiovigilance Programme of India (MvPI); industry bodies welcomed the order (reported 21 May 2024).  
  Source: Business Standard – Medical device bodies welcome CDSCO order on self-reporting adverse effects (2024-05-21) <https://www.business-standard.com/health/medical-device-bodies-welcome-cdsco-order-on-self-reporting-adverse-effects-124052101276_1.html> — Note: Also lexplosion.in and digitalhealthnews.com.

## Post-market – MvPI
- **[likely] F35** MvPI was launched in 2015 with the Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission (IPC) as National Coordination Centre; reporting tools are the Medical Device Adverse Event (MDAE) form, a toll-free helpline, and a Field Safety Corrective Action (FSCA) form used to notify recalls/corrections; training via mvpi-ipc@gov.in.  
  Source: IntechOpen – Materiovigilance chapter <https://intechopen.com/chapters/76231> — Note: Also iasgyan.in and digitalhealthnews.com. Launch date commonly given as 6 July 2015 (unverified).

## Post-market – recall

## Amendments – Aug 2026 package
- **[likely] F44** The August 2026 'simplification' of MDR-2017 reported in the press (The Hindu/Deccan Herald, 25 Aug 2026) comprises: removal of the mandatory loan-licence for outsourced sterilisation where the sterilisation site holds its own MDR licence (Rule 44 labelling change), a standardised testing-fee schedule (Ninth Schedule), and recognition of EU approvals in Rule 63 – i.e. the content of G.S.R. 743(E) and 744(E) notified 14 Aug 2026.
