## USA FDA QMSR

## EU MDR classes
- **[likely] EU-01** EU MDR 2017/745 (fully applicable since 26 May 2021) uses four risk classes: Class I (with sub-types Is sterile, Im measuring, Ir reusable surgical), IIa, IIb and III, assigned by the 22 classification rules in Annex VIII; AIMDs are Class III.

## EU certification time
- **[likely] EU-04** Team-NB 2025 survey: by end-2025 member notified bodies had issued 17,260 MDR+IVDR certificates; MDR applications 25,978 vs MDR certificates issued 13,953. ## EU transition deadlines
- **[confirmed] EU-06** Regulation (EU) 2023/607 extended MDR transition for legacy (MDD/AIMDD) devices: Class III custom-made implantables to 26 May 2026; other Class III and Class IIb implantables (except sutures, staples, dental fillings, braces, crowns, screws, wedges, plates, wires, pins, clips, connectors) to 31 Dec 2027; other Class IIb, Class IIa, Class Is and Im to 31 Dec 2028. Conditions: MDR application lodged with an NB by 26 May 2024 and written agreement signed by 26 Sep 2024; also removed the 'sell-off' date.

## EU targeted revision 2025
- **[likely] EU-08** On 16 Dec 2025 the European Commission published proposals to amend MDR/IVDR: accept broader clinical evidence (equivalence data, literature, real-world evidence, in-silico modelling); drop mandatory clinical investigations for Class IIa implantables unless needed for safety; allow predetermined change control plans without prior NB approval of each change; give EMA a coordinating role (vigilance, market surveillance, borderline/classification, clinical investigations, derogations); 50% fee reductions for micro/small enterprises and orphan-device developers. Now before Parliament and Council.

- **[confirmed] UK-02** The Medical Devices (Post-market Surveillance Requirements) (Amendment) (Great Britain) Regulations 2024 were made 16 Dec 2024 and came into force 16 June 2025 – the first major overhaul of UK MDR 2002 – imposing PMS plans, periodic safety update reports and faster vigilance reporting for devices placed on the GB market (medical devices, IVDs, AIMDs).

## Japan classes
- **[confirmed] JP-01** Japan's PMD Act (Act on Securing Quality, Efficacy and Safety of Pharmaceuticals, Medical Devices, etc.) classifies devices by GHTF-based rules into Class I (extremely low risk – 'general medical devices'), Class II (low risk – 'controlled medical devices'), Class III (medium risk) and Class IV (high risk), with Classes III and IV called 'specially controlled medical devices'. Each device gets a JMDN code.  
  Source: Regulations and Approval/Certification of Medical Devices (PMDA) / MasterControl PMDA glossary (2025) <https://www.pmda.go.jp/english/review-services/reviews/0004.html> — Note: PMDA page URL appeared in results (not fetchable); agrees with https://www.mastercontrol.com/glossary-page/japan-pmda-regulations/, elendilabs and j-mdc. GHTF reference and JMDN from elendilabs/j-mdc (likely).

## Japan routes
- **[confirmed] JP-02** Japan pre-market routes: Class I – notification (todokede) to PMDA; Class II – third-party certification by a Registered Certification Body (RCB) where a certification standard exists, otherwise MHLW approval (shonin) after PMDA review; Class III – RCB certification if a standard exists, otherwise MHLW approval; Class IV – always MHLW approval with PMDA review. Foreign makers must act through a Japanese Marketing Authorization Holder (MAH or designated D-MAH) and register as a Foreign Manufacturer.  
  Source: Regulations and Approval/Certification of Medical Devices (PMDA) / Japan PMDA approval guide (meddeviceguide) / Artixio (2025) <https://www.pmda.go.jp/english/review-services/reviews/0004.html> — Note: Consistent across PMDA listing, https://meddeviceguide.com/blog/japan-pmda-approval-guide and https://www.artixio.com/post/japan-medical-device-regulations-registration-process. Japanese terms (todokede/shonin, D-MAH) from memory.

## China law
- **[confirmed] CN-01** China's top-level device law is the Regulations on the Supervision and Administration of Medical Devices, State Council Order No. 739, effective 1 June 2021 (replacing Order No. 680); eight chapters covering registration/filing, production, distribution and use, adverse events and recall, inspection and liability; introduced a Marketing Authorization Holder (MAH) regime allowing outsourced manufacturing.  
  Source: China Unveils New Medical Device Regulations (Ropes & Gray) / NMPA English site / NAMSA (2021-06-01) <https://www.ropesgray.com/en/newsroom/alerts/2021/March/China-Unveils-New-Medical-Device-Regulations> — Note: NMPA official text listing: https://english.nmpa.gov.cn/2021-05/31/c_644306.htm; also https://namsa.com/china-state-council-releases-order-739/ and Cisema.

## China classes & routes
- **[likely] CN-02** NMPA classes: Class I (low risk) – record filing (beian) only; Class II (moderate risk) – registration reviewed by the provincial Medical Products Administration; Class III (high risk) – registration by central NMPA. All imported devices, whatever the class, file/register with central NMPA. Class II/III need type testing at an NMPA-authorised test centre (self-test reports generally suffice for Class I); technical review target for Class II is 60 working days.  
  Source: Overview of Medical Device Regulation in China (Morgan Lewis) / Emergo China NMPA Registration Whitepaper / Cisema classification guide (2024-12) <https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/overview-of-medical-device-regulation-in-china> — Note: Also https://www.emergobyul.com/sites/default/files/2024-12/China-NMPA-Registration-Whitepaper.pdf and https://cisema.com/en/china-medical-device-risk-classification-guide/. ## China clinical evaluation
- **[likely] CN-03** Clinical evaluation routes in China: Class I needs no clinical data; Class II/III generally require clinical evaluation, which can be satisfied without a local trial if the device is on NMPA's Catalogue of Medical Devices Exempt from Clinical Evaluation/Trials or by equivalence comparison with a marketed device; otherwise a clinical trial in China (or accepted overseas data) is needed. Classification is driven by the NMPA Classification Catalogue.  
  Source: Overview of Medical Device Regulation in China (Morgan Lewis) / PureGlobal China NMPA glossary (2024) <https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/overview-of-medical-device-regulation-in-china> — Note: Also https://www.pureglobal.com/glossary/china-nmpa. ## Australia ARTG & reliance
- **[confirmed] AU-02** Every device must be included in the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG) by an Australian sponsor. Class I (non-sterile, non-measuring) can self-certify against the Essential Principles with a manufacturer's Declaration of Conformity; Class IIa, IIb and III need manufacturer evidence. TGA accepts 'comparable overseas regulator' evidence: EU notified-body CE certificates, US FDA 510(k)/PMA decisions, Health Canada licences, Japan MHLW/PMDA approvals, MDSAP certificates (added 2018) and Singapore HSA registrations (added 2022) – but Australian classification, sponsor and post-market reporting still apply.  
  Source: Use of market authorisation evidence from comparable overseas regulators / assessment bodies for medical devices including IVDs (TGA) / Emergo review / PharmOut (2024-10) <https://www.tga.gov.au/sites/default/files/use-market-authorisation-evidence-comparable-overseas-regulators-assessment-bodies-medical-devices-including-ivds.pdf> — Note: TGA PDF listed (a 2026-05 revised .docx version also appeared: https://www.tga.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-05/use-market-authorisation-evidence-comparable-overseas-regulators-medical-devices-including-ivds.docx). ## Canada classes & licences
- **[likely] CA-01** Health Canada (Medical Devices Regulations SOR/98-282) uses Classes I (low), II (medium), III (high) and IV (maximum risk). Class I needs no device licence but the manufacturer/importer/distributor must hold a Medical Device Establishment Licence (MDEL); Classes II, III and IV need a Medical Device Licence (MDL) with escalating safety/effectiveness evidence (Class IV the most, incl. clinical data). Since January 2026 Class II–IV submissions go through the Regulatory Enrolment Process (REP) and the Common Electronic Submissions Gateway (CESG).  
  Source: Health Canada medical device license MDL / MDALL guide (CASRAI) / Freyr Health Canada registration / PureGlobal Canada (2026-01) <https://www.casrai.org/guides/health-canada-medical-device-license-mdl-mdall> — Note: Also https://www.freyrsolutions.com/medical-devices/health-canada-medical-device-registration and https://www.pureglobal.com/markets/canada; official FAQ page listed: https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/corporate/about-health-canada/legislation-guidelines/acts-regulations/frequently-asked-questions-medical-devices-regulations.html. ## GHTF classification principles

## WHO model framework
- **[confirmed] GL-05** The WHO Global Model Regulatory Framework for Medical Devices including IVDs (2017; ISBN 978-92-4-151235-0; 72 pages) recommends a stepwise path for countries with little or no device regulation: start with 'basic-level' controls (law, regulatory authority, A–D risk classification, establishment registration, listing, vigilance, reliance on other regulators) and progress to 'expanded-level' controls (inspection, conformity assessment, clinical-investigation oversight). ## Classification equivalence (4-tier systems)
- **[likely] MAP-01** Four-tier systems are built on the same GHTF/IMDRF rules, so they map almost one-to-one by risk tier: GHTF A / B / C / D = India A / B / C / D = EU (and UK, Australia) I / IIa / IIb / III = Japan I / II / III / IV = Canada I / II / III / IV. Example: a pacemaker is India D, EU III, Japan IV, Canada IV, US III; an infusion pump is India C, EU IIb, Japan III, Canada III, US II; a syringe is India B, EU IIa, Japan II, Canada II, US II; exam gloves are India A, EU I, Japan I, Canada I, US I.  
  Source: GHTF/SG1/N77:2012 (IMDRF) / Japan classification 'determined by reference to GHTF' (MasterControl, Elendi Labs) / CDSCO Classification PDF (2012-11-02) <https://www.imdrf.org/sites/default/files/docs/ghtf/final/sg1/technical-docs/ghtf-sg1-n77-2012-principles-medical-devices-classification-121102.pdf> — Note: No single official table mapping all jurisdictions was found; this mapping is assembled from GHTF N77 examples (omcmedical.com/ghtf-medical-device-classification), Japan GHTF alignment (https://www.mastercontrol.com/glossary-page/japan-pmda-regulations/, https://elendilabs.com/en/articles/jpn-medical-device-standards) and CDSCO's A–D rules (https://www.cdsco.gov.in/opencms/export/sites/CDSCO_WEB/Pdf-documents/medical-device/Classificationg1.pdf). Caveat (bioaccess, cms.bioaccessla.com/?p=13688): a device's class can still differ between countries because rule wording differs – always classify per jurisdiction.

## Classification equivalence (US 3-tier)
- **[likely] MAP-02** The US FDA's three classes do not map cleanly onto the four-tier GHTF scheme: FDA Class I ≈ GHTF/India A; FDA Class II (510(k)) absorbs most of GHTF B and C (EU IIa and IIb); FDA Class III (PMA) ≈ GHTF D plus some C devices. Classification in the US is by product code in 21 CFR Parts 862–892 rather than by rules, which is why the same device can be EU IIb but US Class II, or EU III but US Class II (e.g., some orthopaedic implants).  
  Source: US FDA and EU Risk Classification of Medical Devices (Emergo by UL) / Mantra Systems 'How EU device classification differs from the US' (2025) <https://www.emergobyul.com/news/us-fda-and-eu-risk-classification-medical-devices> — Note: Also https://mantrasystems.com/articles/how-eu-medical-device-classification-differs-from-the-us-are-you-prepared and https://spyro-soft.com/blog/healthcare/eu-mdr-vs-fda-what-are-the-main-differences-and-similarities.
