Q: 1. FDA final guidance 'Marketing Submission Recommendations for a Predetermined Change Control Plan for Artificial Intelligence-Enabled Device Software Functions': final date, URL, purpose
  [confirmed] Finalised in December 2024. The FDA Roundup of 3 December 2024 lists the final guidance, and law-firm alerts give 4 December 2024 as the release date. It replaced the April 2023 draft and widened the scope from ML-enabled devices to all AI-enabled devices. Purpose in one line: a PCCP should 'describe the planned AI-DSF modifications, the associated methodology to develop, validate, and implement' them, plus an impact assessment. This lets manufacturers make pre-authorised changes without a new submission for each one. It applies to 510(k), De Novo and PMA submissions. FDA held a webinar on the final guidance on 14 January 2025.

Q: 2. FDA draft guidance on pulse oximeters and skin pigmentation (Jan 2025): title, date, key requirement
  [confirmed] Title: 'Pulse Oximeters for Medical Purposes - Non-Clinical and Clinical Performance Testing, Labeling, and Premarket Submission Recommendations' (draft guidance). Q: 4. US investigational device rules: 21 CFR 812 and IRB significant vs non-significant risk determination
  [confirmed] 21 CFR 812 recognises three kinds of device study: significant risk (SR), nonsignificant risk (NSR) and exempt. The sponsor makes the first SR/NSR determination and presents it to the IRB. FDA is 'the final arbiter' of whether a study is SR or NSR. SR studies need an FDA-approved IDE and IRB approval before they start. NSR studies follow the abbreviated requirements in 21 CFR 812.2(b): labelling, IRB approval, informed consent, monitoring, records, reports, and a ban on promotion. They do not need an IDE application to FDA.
  CITE: US FDA. Information Sheet Guidance for IRBs, Clinical Investigators, and Sponsors: Significant Risk and Nonsignificant Risk Medical Device Studies; 21 CFR Part 812 (Investigational Device Exemptions).
  URLS: https://www.fda.gov/files/about%20fda/published/Significant-Risk-and-Nonsignificant-Risk-Medical-Device-Studies---Information-Sheet.pdf ; https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/investigational-device-exemption-ide/ide-approval-process ; https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/investigational-device-exemption-ide/faqs-about-investigational-device-exemption
  ! The information sheet is an older FDA guidance document (circa 2006). It is still the standard reference.
Q: 5. EU MDR 2017/745: transition deadlines extended by Regulation (EU) 2023/607; 2025-2026 Commission simplification proposal; PMCF requirement
  [confirmed] Regulation (EU) 2023/607 entered into force on 20 March 2023. It extended the transition period from 26 May 2024 to 31 December 2027 for class III and class IIb implantable devices, and to 31 December 2028 for other class IIb, class IIa, Im, Is and Ir devices. The extension applies only to legacy devices with valid MDD/AIMDD certificates, and a written agreement with a notified body had to be signed by 26 September 2024. On 16 December 2025 the Commission proposed a 'targeted' revision of MDR/IVDR (COM(2025) 1023 final, with SWD(2025) 1050). It aims to simplify classification, streamline clinical evidence requirements, reduce notified-body involvement, and update the rules for digital, AI and cybersecurity. As of mid-2026 (June 2026 reports) it was still being negotiated by Parliament and Council. PMCF: Article 61 and Annex XIV Part B require a PMCF plan that proactively collects and evaluates clinical data within the post-market surveillance system. For class III and implantable devices, the PMCF evaluation report and the SSCP must be updated 'at least annually'.
  CITE: Regulation (EU) 2023/607 amending Regulations (EU) 2017/745 and (EU) 2017/746 as regards transitional provisions; European Commission COM(2025) 1023 final (16 Dec 2025); Regulation (EU) 2017/745, Art. 61 and Annex XIV Part B.

Q: 6. UK Cumberlege Review 'First Do No Harm' (8 July 2020): key recommendations
  [confirmed] Published 8 July 2020 by the Independent Medicines and Medical Devices Safety Review, chaired by Baroness Julia Cumberlege. It covered Primodos (hormone pregnancy tests), sodium valproate and pelvic mesh, and made 9 strategic recommendations and 50 actions for improvement. These include:
- a government apology;
- an independent Patient Safety Commissioner, described as 'the patients' port of call';
- a Redress Agency for those harmed, using a non-adversarial process, plus separate schemes for HPTs, valproate and mesh;
- a central database recording the patient, the implanted device and the surgeon;
- an expanded GMC register listing all doctors' 'financial and non-pecuniary interests'.
  CITE: Independent Medicines and Medical Devices Safety Review (Cumberlege J, chair). First Do No Harm. 8 July 2020.
  URLS: https://www.immdsreview.org.uk/downloads/IMMDSReview_Web.pdf ; https://www.immdsreview.org.uk/Report.html ; https://www.bmj.com/content/381/bmj.p960
  ! The UK government later declined to fund a redress agency (BMJ 2023 report). Present redress as 'recommended' and say whether it was implemented, not 'established'.
Q: 7. US Physician Payments Sunshine Act / CMS Open Payments
  [confirmed] Open Payments is a federal transparency programme. It collects and publishes payments and transfers of value from manufacturers of covered drugs, devices, biologicals and medical supplies to physicians and teaching hospitals. Applicable manufacturers report every year: consulting fees, honoraria, food, travel, research payments, royalties, and ownership or investment interests. CMS publishes the data in a searchable form each year.
  CITE: Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services. Open Payments (Physician Payments Sunshine Act) Fact Sheet for Physicians / Applicable Manufacturers.
  URLS: https://www.cms.gov/files/document/physician-fact-sheetpdf ; https://www.cms.gov/files/document/applicable-manufacturer-fact-sheetpdf ; https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/affordable-care-act-sunshine-rule-increases-transparency-health-care
  ! Since 2021 (data year), certain non-physician practitioners such as PAs and NPs are also covered recipients. That was not checked in these searches.

Q: A2. Daphnet FoG dataset: Bachlin M et al., IEEE Trans Inf Technol Biomed 2010. Is the citation correct, with n=10 participants?

On-body accelerometers (ankle, thigh, lower back) detected FOG from frequency components and triggered a rhythmic auditory cue. 'Ten Parkinson's disease patients' did walking tasks in the laboratory. Q: B4. Cochrane review Li S et al. 2013, 'Electromagnetic fields for treating osteoarthritis': main conclusion and certainty

Q: B6. Guideline positions on PEMF and electrotherapy for knee OA: OARSI 2019, ACR/Arthritis Foundation 2019, NICE NG226 (2022)

The list: TENS, ultrasound therapy, interferential therapy, laser therapy, pulsed short-wave therapy, NMES. Evidence review G covers electrotherapy. NET: no major guideline recommends PEMF or electrotherapy for knee OA, and OARSI explicitly recommends strongly against electromagnetic therapy.

Q: B8. Minimal clinically important improvement (MCII) for VAS pain in knee OA (Tubach F et al. 2005, Ann Rheum Dis)
  [confirmed] CONFIRMED: prospective multicentre 4-week cohort of 1362 outpatients. MCII estimated in 814 patients (603 knee OA, 211 hip OA) using a patient-anchored 5-point Likert response. For KNEE OA: MCII for pain was -19.9 mm (-40.8%) on a 0-100 mm VAS; patient global assessment -18.3 mm (-39.0%); WOMAC function -9.1 (-26.0%). RELEVANCE TO SwaKnee: a ~32% mean relative VAS reduction in the active arm is below the 40.8% relative MCII. The between-arm difference of about 18 percentage points (32% vs 14%) is also below the ~19.9 mm absolute threshold, if baseline VAS were about 100 mm or less. So a statistically significant difference may not mean individual patients improved by a clinically important amount.

Q: 1b. WMA Declaration of Helsinki 2024: which paragraph covers post-trial provisions?
  [confirmed] This is Paragraph 34, headed 'Post-Trial Provisions'. In advance of a clinical trial, sponsors and researchers must arrange post-trial provisions. These may be provided by the sponsors and researchers themselves, by healthcare systems or by governments. They cover all participants who still need an intervention 'identified as beneficial and reasonably safe in the trial'. A research ethics committee must approve any exception. Specific information about post-trial provisions must be disclosed to participants as part of informed consent. Short quote: 'post-trial provisions must be arranged by sponsors and researchers'.
  CITE: World Medical Association. Declaration of Helsinki (2024 revision), paragraph 34.

Q: 3. Lundh A et al. Cochrane 2017, 'Industry sponsorship and research outcome': key results
  [confirmed] Compared with non-industry sponsored studies:
- Favourable efficacy results were more common in industry-sponsored studies: RR 1.27 (95% CI 1.17 to 1.37); 25 papers; moderate-quality evidence.

Q: 4. Institute of Medicine 2009: core definition of conflict of interest
  [confirmed] The IOM defines a conflict of interest as a set of circumstances that 'creates a risk that professional judgment or actions regarding a primary interest' will be 'unduly influenced by a secondary interest'. Primary interests include the integrity of research, the welfare of patients and the quality of medical education. Secondary interests include financial gain and also the desire for professional advancement, recognition for personal achievement, and favours to friends, family, students or colleagues. The definition is on pp. 38-39 of the report, released 28 April 2009.

Q: 5. Therapeutic misconception (Appelbaum et al. 1982 and 1987): definition and citation
  [confirmed] Appelbaum, Roth and Lidz first described the concept in 1982, after structured interviews with participants in psychiatric research. The 1987 paper gives the classic formulation: holding a therapeutic misconception means failing to recognise that research procedures, such as randomisation, protocol constraints and placebo, are not individualised care. In their words, it is 'to deny the possibility that there may be major disadvantages to participating' in research.

Q: 6. GMC (UK) 'Making and using visual and audio recordings of patients': consent rules for teaching and publicity, and the date of the current version
  [confirmed] The guidance came into effect on 9 May 2011 and was last updated on 18 December 2024. The update reflected the new research guidance and the regulation of physician associates and anaesthesia associates.
