Q: 5. Uniform Code for Marketing Practices in Medical Devices (UCMPMD) 2024: date, key provisions, legal status, official URL
  [confirmed] Dates: the Department of Pharmaceuticals notified the code by Circular F. No. 31026/23/2022-Policy dated 6.9.2024. It was amended by Circular No. 1 of 2024 (F. No. 31026/44/2024-MD, dated 4.10.2024). Later amendments appear in a 2025 circular and in Circular 1-2026, with the code 'as amended on 30.4.2026'. Gifts: no gift for the personal benefit of healthcare professionals or their family members (immediate and extended), given by device companies or their agents. Travel and hospitality: no travel or hospitality such as foreign trips. Claims: product information must be accurate, balanced, not misleading, and 'capable of substantiation', with substantiation provided to HCPs on request. Free evaluation samples are valued under set rules, and companies must disclose spending on medical education, conferences and free samples and file annual self-declarations of compliance. Complaints go to an Ethics Committee for Marketing Practices in Medical Devices (ECMPMD) in each industry association, chaired by the association's CEO with 3-5 members, which must decide complaints within 90 days. Legal status: a voluntary, self-regulatory code with no statutory backing, run through industry associations. Commentators note the government may consider making it statutory if companies do not comply.

Q: 7a. NMC RMP (Professional Conduct) Regulations 2023 held in abeyance Aug 2023; current rule on doctor-industry relationships (IMC 2002 clause 6.8)
  [confirmed] The NMC RMP (Professional Conduct) Regulations 2023 were published on 2 Aug 2023. By gazette notification on 23 Aug 2023, the NMC Ethics Board said they would 'not be operative and effective till further Gazette Notification'. NMC adopted the Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002 with immediate effect. The hold followed concerns raised by the IMA, notably about mandatory generic prescribing. Clause 6.8 of the 2002 regulations was inserted by an MCI amendment of 10 Dec 2009. It is a 'Code of conduct for doctors ... with pharmaceutical and allied health sector industry'. It bars doctors from receiving gifts. It bars travel facilities for self and family, inside or outside the country, including for conferences, seminars, workshops and CMEs as a delegate. Clause 6.8.1(c) also covers hospitality.

Q: 1b. Which ICMR 2017 section covers medical device research?
  [confirmed] Device trials are in Section 7 (Clinical trials of drugs and other interventions), subsection 7.7 'Device trials'. That subsection defines a medical device as one not achieving its primary action by pharmacological, immunological or metabolic means. It says device trials follow these guidelines, Indian GCP and GSR 78(E) dated 31.1.2017 (MDR-2017). It also says devices should be provided free of cost or, if expensive, at feasible reduced rates. ICMR has separately issued a draft 'Ethical Guidance for Non-Regulatory Clinical Trials', which says non-regulatory clinical trials start only after Ethics Committee approval.
  CITE: ICMR. National Ethical Guidelines 2017, Section 7.7 (Device trials); ICMR. Draft Ethical Guidance for Non-Regulatory Clinical Trials (draft v2).

Q: 1c. ICMR 2017 provisions on conflict of interest and post-trial access
  [confirmed] Conflict of interest is defined as conditions where 'professional judgement concerning a primary interest tends to be unduly influenced by a secondary interest'. Q: 1d. ICMR 2017 informed consent: vulnerable participants and audio-visual consent
  [confirmed] Section 5 is 'Informed consent process' and Section 6 is 'Vulnerability'. Consent can be documented by audio or video recording, with the participant, the PI and the impartial witness all in frame. An impartial literate witness, not connected to the research, is needed when the participant or LAR is illiterate. The legal AV requirement comes from the 2013 fifth amendment to the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules (now carried into the NDCT Rules). It requires AV recording of consent for vulnerable participants in NCE/NME trials. Anti-HIV and anti-leprosy drug trials need audio recording only.
  CITE: ICMR 2017 Guidelines, Section 5 (Informed consent process) and Section 6 (Vulnerability); institutional summaries (Goa University; KEM Hospital IEC SOP).
