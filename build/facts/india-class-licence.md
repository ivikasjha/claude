## Classification - Rule 4
- **[confirmed] F01** Rule 4(1) MDR-2017: medical devices other than IVDs are classified on the risk parameters in Part I of the First Schedule as (i) low risk - Class A; (ii) low moderate risk - Class B; (iii) moderate high risk - Class C; (iv) high risk - Class D. Rule 4(2): IVDs are classified on Part II of the First Schedule into the same four classes.  
  Source: Mondaq - DCGI notifies the medical devices and IVD classification under MDR 2017 (2017-01-31) <https://mondaq.com/india/food-and-drugs-law/668254/dcgi-notifies-the-medical-devices-and-in-vitro-diagnostic-ivd-medical-devices-classification-under-medical-device-rule-2017> — Note: Same wording also in AZB Partners summary (https://azbpartners.com/bank/medical-devices-rules-2017) and CDSCO IVD FAQ (cdsco.gov.in CDSCO-IVD-FAQ-03-2022). All cdsco.gov.in fetches were egress-blocked; wording taken from search-result text.

## Classification - First Schedule parameters
- **[likely] F02** First Schedule Part I groups rules by: non-invasive devices; invasive devices (body-orifice vs surgically invasive; transient / short-term / long-term); active devices (therapeutic, diagnostic, administering/removing substances); plus special rules. Class A = non-invasive devices not contacting the patient or touching intact skin only, and devices for collecting/storing specimens; Class B = short-term invasive devices, active therapeutic devices administering/exchanging energy, non-invasive devices channelling/storing blood or body fluids; Class C = long-term surgically invasive/implantable devices, active devices administering medicines/energy in a potentially hazardous way, devices monitoring vital physiological processes; Class D = long-term surgically invasive devices in contact with the heart, central circulatory system or CNS, devices incorporating a medicinal product, devices made from animal tissues.  
  Source: Bhatt & Joshi Associates - Medical Device Regulations India 2026: CDSCO, MDR 2017, Compliance Guide (2026) <https://bhattandjoshiassociates.com/medical-device-regulation-in-india-a-comprehensive-overview/> — Note: Consultancy paraphrase of the First Schedule (which mirrors GHTF/EU rules). Official PDF (cdsco.gov.in MDR2017.pdf, main.mohfw.gov.in) could not be fetched (egress blocked / DNS failure). The Schedule's exact rule numbering and the software rule ('software that drives or influences a device falls in the same class as the device') are from memory and UNVERIFIED.

## Licensing authority by class
- **[confirmed] F13** Manufacturing licences for Class A and Class B devices are granted by the State Licensing Authority (SLA); manufacturing licences for Class C and Class D and import licences for ALL classes are granted by the Central Licensing Authority (CLA = DCGI/CDSCO).  
  Source: AZB Partners - Medical Devices Rules 2017 (2017-04-01) <https://azbpartners.com/bank/medical-devices-rules-2017> — Note: Also CDSCO 'RegulatoryMDR-2017' overview PDF (https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/export/sites/CDSCO_WEB/Pdf-documents/medical-device/RegulatoryMDR-2017.pdf) and RegisterKaro.

## Class A non-sterile non-measuring - 2022 amendment
- **[confirmed] F20** Medical Devices (Sixth Amendment) Rules 2022, G.S.R. 777(E) dated 14 October 2022 (draft G.S.R. 710(E), 20 Sept 2022): Class A NON-sterile and NON-measuring devices are exempted from the manufacturing-licence regime; instead the manufacturer registers on the 'Online System for Medical Devices' by uploading (i) name and address of the manufacturing site, (ii) details of the devices, (iii) an undertaking that the device is Class A non-sterile non-measuring, and (iv) a self-certification of conformity with the Essential Principles checklist.  
  Source: TeamLease RegTech - Medical Devices (Sixth Amendment) Rules, 2022 (2022-10-14) <https://www.teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/19622/medical-devices-sixth-amendment-rules-2022> — Note: Also Corpbiz (https://corpbiz.io/learning/medical-devices-sixth-amendment-rules-2022/) and Lexplosion. No specific 'Form MD-xx' number appeared for this registration; the portal issues a registration number. Sterile or measuring Class A devices still need the MD-5 licence.

## Timelines - Class A manufacture (Rule 20)
- **[likely] F25** Class A manufacturing licence (MD-5): the SLA grants the licence after document scrutiny within 45 days of application WITHOUT a prior audit; the registered notified body then audits the site within 120 days of the grant.  
  Source: Applied Clinical Trials - New Regulations for Medical Devices in India (2017) <https://www.appliedclinicaltrialsonline.com/view/new-regulations-medical-devices-india> — Note: Consistent with S.S. Exact sub-rule numbers not verified.

## Timelines - Class B manufacture (Rule 20)
- **[likely] F26** Class B manufacturing licence: the registered notified body must audit the manufacturing site within 90 days of the application, BEFORE the SLA grants the licence; the SLA then grants or rejects after receiving the audit report.  
  Source: Applied Clinical Trials - New Regulations for Medical Devices in India (2017) <https://www.appliedclinicaltrialsonline.com/view/new-regulations-medical-devices-india> — Note: The number of days the SLA has after receiving the audit report (memory: 45 days) was not confirmed. ## Licence validity and retention fee
- **[confirmed] F30** MDR-2017 licences are perpetual: a manufacturing or import licence stays valid indefinitely with no renewal application, provided the licence retention fee in the Second Schedule is paid every 5 years from the date of issue (unless suspended or cancelled).  
  Source: Lawrbit - Class A & B medical device manufacturing licence India (2017) <https://www.lawrbit.com/industry-specific/class-a-b-medical-device-manufacturing-license-india/> — Note: Also AZB Partners, ELP Law and CDSCO retention-fee notice coverage (Lexplosion, Digital Health News). Rule numbers from memory: Rule 26 (manufacture) and Rule 38 (import) - UNVERIFIED.

## Retention fee - late fee and deemed cancellation
- **[likely] F31** If the retention fee is not paid on time, a late fee of 2% of the fee per month applies; if it is still unpaid after the grace window - 180 days for manufacturing licences, 90 days for import licences - the licence is deemed cancelled. CDSCO has issued public notices urging licence holders to pay retention fees on time.  
  Source: Lexplosion - Stakeholders in medical devices sector urged by CDSCO to ensure timely payment of retention fees (2024) <https://lexplosion.in/stakeholders-in-medical-devices-sector-urged-by-cdsco-to-ensure-timely-payment-of-retention-fees-to-maintain-validity-of-licenses-and-certificates/> — Note: Also https://www.artixio.com/post/cdsco-medical-device-licence-retention and https://www.digitalhealthnews.com/cdsco-reminds-medical-device-cos-to-complete-retention-fee-to-keep-licenses-valid. ## Notified bodies - rule and role
- **[confirmed] F32** Rule 13 MDR-2017: notified bodies register with the Central Licensing Authority to audit manufacturing sites of Class A and Class B devices for conformity with the Quality Management System (Fifth Schedule) and applicable standards; they may also be called on to assist with Class C and D regulation. Notified bodies must be accredited by NABCB (National Accreditation Board for Certification Bodies, Quality Council of India).  
  Source: CDSCO FAQ on Medical Devices 2018 (FAQmd2018.pdf) and NABCB note 'Health Ministry Notifies Medical Devices Rules, 2017' (2018) <https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/resources/UploadCDSCOWeb/2018/UploadImmunization/FAQmd2018.pdf> — Note: NABCB document: https://nabcb.qci.org.in/documents/NABCB%20to%20Accredit%20Notified%20Bodies%20for%20Medical%20Devices.pdf ; TUV Rheinland blog confirms the NABCB route.

## Phase-in - S.O. 648(E)
- **[confirmed] F34** S.O. 648(E) dated 11 February 2020 (MoHFW) notified ALL medical devices - 'all devices including an instrument, apparatus, appliance, implant, material or other article... intended for use in human beings or animals' - as drugs under s.3(b)(iv) of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act 1940, with effect from 1 April 2020.  
  Source: CDSCO - File No.29/Misc/03/2022-DC(257) notice on S.O. 648(E)/G.S.R. 102(E) (mdgsr.pdf); CDSCO Medical Devices Rules page (2020-02-11) <https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/export/sites/CDSCO_WEB/Pdf-documents/mdgsr.pdf> — Note: Also https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/opencms/en/Acts-and-rules/Medical-Devices-Rules/ and Khaitan & Co (https://khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/All-medical-devices-notified-to-be-regulated).

## Phase-in - G.S.R. 102(E) Chapter IIIA
- **[confirmed] F35** G.S.R. 102(E) dated 11 February 2020 (Medical Devices (Amendment) Rules 2020) inserted Chapter IIIA 'Registration of Certain Medical Devices' (Rule 19A onward): every device newly covered by S.O. 648(E) - except those already listed in the Annexure to the Eighth Schedule - must be registered with the CLA through the 'Online System for Medical Devices'; registration was voluntary for 18 months from 1 April 2020 and mandatory thereafter (i.e., from 1 October 2021); the registration number must appear on the label.  
  Source: LegitQuest - Medical Devices (Amendment) Rules, 2020 (text); Medical Dialogues - Gazette calls for registrations of medical devices (2020-02-11) <https://www.legitquest.com/act/medical-devices-amendment-rules-2020/9813> — Note: Medical Dialogues: https://business.medicaldialogues.in/medical-devices/gazette-calls-for-registrations-of-medical-devices-63105 ; USISPF summary: https://usispf.org/newsletter-weekly/ministry-of-health-family-welfare-publishes-the-g-s-r-102-e-for-regulation-of-all-class-c-d-medical-devices

## Phase-in - licensing dates
- **[confirmed] F36** Under G.S.R. 102(E) the exemption from licensing for newly notified devices ended after 30 months for Class A and B (licence mandatory from 1 October 2022) and after 42 months for Class C and D (licence mandatory from 1 October 2023), counted from 1 April 2020.  
  Source: LegitQuest - Medical Devices (Amendment) Rules, 2020; Goa FDA notice; S.S. Rana (2020-02-11) <https://www.legitquest.com/act/medical-devices-amendment-rules-2020/9813> — Note: Also https://dfda.goa.gov.in/?p=5065 and https://ssrana.in/articles/class-c-and-d-non-notified-medical-devices-to-require-licensing-under-medical-devices-rules-2017-from-october-1-2023/ . Timeline for slide: 1 Apr 2020 all devices regulated / voluntary registration -> 1 Oct 2021 mandatory registration -> 1 Oct 2022 Class A/B licence -> 1 Oct 2023 Class C/D licence.

## Class A self-certification principle
- **[likely] F43** Under MDR-2017 Class A manufacturers obtain the licence on self-certification of compliance; the notified body audits the site after grant and certifies the QMS. Class A and B licences are granted by SLAs after/with notified-body QMS certification, whereas Class C and D sites are inspected by CDSCO Medical Device Officers.  
  Source: iPleaders - Need to know Medical Devices Rules 2017 (2017) <https://blog.ipleaders.in/need-know-medical-devices-rules-2017/> — Note: Consistent with F25/F26/F27.

## Registration v licence - Eighth Schedule exclusion
- **[likely] F44** Devices already notified before 2020 (listed in the Annexure to the Eighth Schedule, e.g., syringes, stents, condoms, IUDs, catheters, orthopaedic implants, heart valves) were never covered by the Chapter IIIA registration window - they needed full MDR-2017 licences from 1 January 2018.

## MDR-2017 basics
- **[likely] F45** The Medical Devices Rules 2017 were notified by MoHFW on 31 January 2017 (G.S.R. 78(E)) under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act 1940 and came into force on 1 January 2018; they replaced regulation of devices as 'drugs' under the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules 1945 with a risk-based, GHTF-aligned framework.  
  Source: Medical Dialogues - Ministry of Health notifies Medical Device Rules 2017, here are 5 major takeaways (2017-01-31) <https://business.medicaldialogues.in/ministry-of-health-notifies-medical-device-rules-2017-here-are-5-major-takeaways> — Note: G.S.R. 78(E) number and 1 Jan 2018 commencement are from memory; the 2017 notification itself is on cdsco.gov.in (MDR2017.pdf) which could not be fetched.

## Deemed approval
- **[likely] F46** No general 'deemed approval' for manufacturing licences exists in MDR-2017; the only deemed-grant mechanism found is in Rule 87A (Fifth Amendment 2022), where the SLA must reject an MD-41 application within 10 days, failing which the MD-42 registration certificate is granted.  
  Source: Corpbiz - Medical Devices (Fifth Amendment) Rules 2022 (2022-09-30) <https://corpbiz.io/learning/medical-devices-fifth-amendment-rules-2022/> — Note: Statement that no deemed approval exists for MD-5/MD-9 is an inference from all sources reviewed; the 2026 draft amendment discussion mentions timelines but no deemed approval.
